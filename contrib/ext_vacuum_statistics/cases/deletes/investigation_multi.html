A 4-PROBLEM INVESTIGATION

Vacuum misconfigs caught from per-relation statistics

Custom multi-table pgbench workload (16 clients, 600 s per phase) on pgbench_accounts/tellers/history. Same workload, four misconfig flavours, one healthy baseline — told the way Andrey Zubkov reads pg_profile reports.

RUN CONTEXT

How the run actually went.

Workload: custom pattern_multi_user.sql — per-transaction UPDATE pgbench_accounts + UPDATE pgbench_tellers + INSERT pgbench_history; 16 clients scale = 50 4 phases × 600 s × 2 modes. Stopped early in fixed mode (interrupt+wraparound got partial fixed-side coverage). Cumulative wall-clock time across phases: 69 min total transactions executed: 34 487.

Cluster context: debug-build PostgreSQL (running from my_postgres7 source tree, likely --enable-debug --enable-cassert) — assertion overhead caps per-transaction throughput at single-digit TPS even with 16 clients. That's why pgbench TPS in the table below stays in the 5–14 range instead of the 1 000+ you'd see on a release build. The signals are still valid — they just take longer to accumulate.
