VACUUM misconfigurations — explainer

Two scenarios laid out in three layers: Setup (what differs), Mechanism (the causal chain inside vacuum), Outcome (numbers + cumulative chart). The Insight at the end is the one thing to take away.

A — Small maintenance_work_mem breaks VACUUM scalability

sparse DELETE on pgbench_accounts (~62 500 unique aids), explicit VACUUM at end of phase — numbers from out_broken/vac_broken_A-sparse.log

2 · Mechanism — the causal chain inside VACUUM

maintenance_work_mem = 64 kB↓dead-TID buffer holds ~11 000 entries (≈ 64 kB / 6 B per entry)↓phase accumulates ~62 500 dead pointers across the heap↓each buffer fill → full B-tree pass → buffer reset → repeat↓VACUUM logs 1 430 index scans (it kept slicing, never finished in one go)↓every pass dirties tens of pages → torrent of full-page-image WAL↓→ 743 MB WAL, 270 s wall-clock — vacuum becomes the workload

Vacuum cost scales with the number of index passes, not with the number of dead tuples. A small maintenance_work_mem amplifies a single-shot cleanup into a runaway long-tail vacuum.

E — vacuum_index_cleanup=off blocks the index pass + heap reclaim

sparse DELETE on pgbench_accounts with autovacuum disabled during the phase, explicit VACUUM at end — numbers from out_*/vac_*_E-sparse.log

2 · Mechanism — the causal chain inside VACUUM

vacuum_index_cleanup = off on the table↓VACUUM skips the index pass entirely — forced bypass, no matter how much is dead↓vac_log: index scan bypassed: 62500 pages have 62500 dead item identifiers↓without removing index entries first, VACUUM can't turn heap LP_DEAD → LP_UNUSED↓FIXED with same workload + auto cleanup: vac_log: index scan needed: 62500 pages had 62500 dead item identifiers removed
